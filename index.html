Filed work

Legal work comes first: four systems for a U.S. collections law firm. The code belongs to the client, so each folder tells the story; I'm glad to walk through any of them on a call.

Bank statement reader

In daily use

Staff read every statement line by line and typed each payer into a spreadsheet, the way bank clerks kept ledgers a century ago.

Now a Python reader handles each bank's layout, n8n applies the firm's rules, and a review page lets the team approve, fix or reject each payer. Approved rows reach the case trackers on their own. About 400 tests, nightly backups, and every correction becomes next week's fix.

Registered agent research

Pilot

The firm needed the official address for legal papers for about 500 companies, found one search at a time.

Free government data goes first, then a page reader, then an LLM agent with web search for whatever is left. Every answer carries its source and a quote, a person approves each one, and only a company name and state ever leave the firm. I chose the model by testing four on known answers. Pilot: 21 of 24 found.

Legal notice builder

Co-built

Someone found the case, checked the tax ID by hand, and copied every detail into a template.

I built the first version with a teammate who now runs it: each payer is pre-screened with a plain reason, matched to its case without guessing, checked against the case documents, and drafted as a Doc and PDF for an attorney to review. A log makes sure nothing is drafted twice, and nothing is sent automatically.

PDF tools for the team

In daily use

Court packets were assembled with a desktop app each person had to install.

Now it happens in the browser: the judgment goes into the right page of every document, blank pages are added so packets print double-sided, and large files are compressed under the court's upload limit. Nothing is kept on the server afterwards.
